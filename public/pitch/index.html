Songs are the hard part — yours hold up. The picture around them is what stops the scroll. That’s my half if you want it: one single, photos, cover, video, keepable object.

One trip when it suits us. Yes, later, or never — all fine.

01

The photoshoot

A day around the city, shot on film.

We spend a day hitting the real New York — your busking corners, a rooftop, a 2am subway platform, a greasy-spoon window. I shoot it warm and grainy, the way the record sounds.

You walk away with a set of press shots that make you look like the artist you are, plus the cover frame for the single.

02

The artwork

A hand-made cover. Not a template.

I paint / design the single's cover myself — a piece that could hang on a wall, not a filter on a photo. That's the image that follows the song everywhere: streaming, the poster, the merch, the shirt.

03

The music video

I direct it, shoot it, and cut it. Around NYC.

This is the big one. A real video for the single — I handle it end to end so it costs you nothing but a few days and your face.

  • The city as the lonesome character: ferries, fire escapes, empty diners, the last train.
  • One long performance take woven through the locations — you and the guitar, no lip-sync gloss.
  • Shot run-and-gun and analog, so it looks handmade and expensive at the same time.
/ why it matters

A single with a real video is the difference between "a guy posted a song" and "there's an artist in New York you need to hear." It's the one asset that works on every platform at once — and it doubles as a month of short-form clips.

04

The release look

One world — the site, the type, the colour, all of it.

I build the whole visual identity around the record so it feels like one thing: the website, the cover, the video, the posters, the socials all speak the same language. You already have the start of it — I take it the rest of the way for the release.

05

The wow object

Something people keep — not a throwaway card. NFC picks are cool but pricey at scale; we pick one hero later.

Ideas on the table (mix and match when we’re ready — none of this is locked):

  • Printed lyric / photo card — thick stock, your cover on the front, QR to the song on the back. Cheap, beautiful, pocketable.
  • Playable pick with art — real pick, name + QR on a tiny sleeve or stamped case. Feels like a gift, not promo.
  • 3D-printed object — once Jon’s printer is up: a small guitar-head silhouette, mini amp, or token with your mark; matte black / bone, QR card tucked under. Handmade, limited, not mass-NFC.
  • Case-lid storefront — one laminated board for the street: big QR (listen), Venmo, “playing here a while.” The object can be the whole pitch, not only the handout.
  • Optional NFC later — stick a cheap chip on the best object only if it still feels magic after the art is right. Not the default, not a hundred of them on day one.
/ the point

Strangers should leave with something they don’t trash and a way back to the song. Form follows that — pick, card, print, token — we choose after we see the single’s face.
